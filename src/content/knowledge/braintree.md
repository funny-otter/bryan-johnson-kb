# Braintree

Braintree was the payments company founded by [bryan johnson](/knowledge/bryan-johnson/) in 2007 and acquired by eBay/PayPal in 2013 for $800M. The exit funded Johnson's later work in [os fund](/knowledge/os-fund/), [kernel](/knowledge/kernel/), [blueprint](/knowledge/blueprint/), and [dont die](/knowledge/dont-die/).

## Origin story

CNBC reports Johnson discovered the payments opportunity while selling credit-card processing door-to-door after struggling financially. He described payments as a broken industry with an opportunity to build an exceptional company.

Chicago Booth reports Braintree's growth through developer-friendly payment processing and its later acquisition by PayPal/eBay. Johnson's post-exit question, "How can I help humanity thrive?", led to his deep-tech and human-enhancement projects.

## Early customers and the long tail

On October 2, 2026, Johnson publicly credited Lütke and DHH as Braintree’s “first customers”—“they gave my company life in those early uncertain days”—while supporting them at the Motul Petit Le Mans race weekend. The claim is consistent with documented early Braintree client lists, which included 37signals (DHH’s company, with co-founder Jason Fried also named in Johnson’s post) and Shopify among marquee word-of-mouth merchants. The same post draws the arc explicitly: a 15-year founder friendship that began as a payments vendor relationship and now surfaces as Johnson reviewing Lütke’s race performance “via biomarkers.” Treat the “first customers” ordering as Johnson’s attribution; the verifiable fact is that both companies were early, publicly named Braintree merchants.
