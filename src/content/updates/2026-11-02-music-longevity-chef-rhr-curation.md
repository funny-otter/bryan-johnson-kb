- Promoted the music-longevity relay with the meta-analysis verified: “Music is a longevity therapy / > lowers heart rate / > lowers blood pressure / > lowers cortisol / > lowers anxiety and restlessness / Protocol: a song you love. / 104 randomized trials / 9,617 people” (2,099 likes, 149 reposts, 126 replies, with an attached figure). Verified: de Witte, Spruit, van Hooren, Moonen, Stams, “Effects of music interventions on stress-related outcomes: a systematic review and two meta-analyses,” Health Psychology Review, June 2020;14(2):294-324 (10.1080/17437199.2019.1627897; PubMed 31167611) — two multilevel meta-analyses of exactly 104 RCTs with exactly 9,617 participants (327 effect sizes), finding music interventions reduced physiological stress outcomes (d = .380) and psychological stress outcomes (d = .545), with all four of the post’s claimed benefits mapping onto measured outcomes: heart rate (d = .456), stress-related hormone levels including cortisol (d = .349), blood pressure (d = .343), and anxiety/restlessness-type psychological outcomes (d = .545). Boundaries recorded: the meta-analysis measures stress reduction, not longevity or lifespan — no included trial tracked mortality, disease, or aging endpoints, so “longevity therapy” is gloss the paper does not make; the pooled effects are small-to-medium (d = .34–.55) and heterogeneous across settings; the paper’s own limitation is that the evidence base is mostly short-term, often single-session interventions, so long-term effects are largely unexamined; “lowers cortisol” narrows the paper’s broader “stress-related hormone levels”; the post names no study; and the claim arrived at the end of a same-day music thread primed by a 4,492-like joy post and a 668-like engagement question (583 replies).
