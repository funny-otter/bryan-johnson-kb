- Promoted the menopause-cliff relay with the study verified: “Menopause was thought to be a gradual decline. This new study of 300M lab results suggests it may be closer to a cliff.” (426 likes, 14 reposts, 35 replies; November 4, 17:04 UTC, with a figure). Verified: Pridham, Hayut, Lavi-Shoseyov, Neeman, Hovav, Toledano, Alon, “Population-scale reconstruction of menopause dynamics reveals a physiological cliff at the final menstrual period,” Nature Medicine, October 12, 2026 (10.1038/s41591-026-04614-0, open access) — an astronomy-inspired temporal deconvolution applied to two cohorts (NHANES 1999–2022 and Israel’s Clalit, 1.3 million females 30–70) totaling 300 million laboratory tests, finding step-like jumps at the final menstrual period in 52/90 US tests (0.1–1.4 s.d.) across endocrine, skeletal, hepatic, renal, inflammatory, and lipid systems, absent in males, concordant across populations (r = 0.89), preceded by sex-hormone dysregulation ~12–15 years before FMP, and attenuated in self-reported menopausal-hormone-therapy users. Boundaries recorded: “300M lab results” is 300 million tests, not patients; the study is three weeks old and uses retrospective cross-sectional data with self-reported menopause status — its own limitations — and Australian menopause clinicians publicly pushed back within a day (“unnecessarily alarming,” “physiological cliff is not a scientific or medical term,” SWAN indicates a roughly three-year gradual transition); the MHT attenuation is associational with healthy-user bias. Connects to the tracked Kate Tolo female-protocol storyline.
- Promoted the DGLA zombie-cell relay with the paper verified: “Zombie cells may have a kill switch / … They're called ‘zombie’ (senescent) cells… They found that these zombie cells run low on one fatty acid: DGLA. So they added it back causing many of the zombie cells to die while the healthy cells survived. They died by ferroptosis where the cell’s outer membrane oxidizes and breaks. / Would be interesting if replicated in humans.” (589 likes, 31 reposts, 47 replies; November 4, 17:46 UTC). Verified: Kuehnemann, Jezak, Mogck, … Vaittinen, … Pihlajamäki, … Wiley, “Fatty acid desaturation controls cellular fate decisions between senescence and ferroptosis,” Cell 2026 (10.1016/j.cell.2026.09.036; Buck Institute-led with University of Eastern Finland, announced November 3, 2026 — one day before the post): senescent cells run low on DGLA (20:3 ω-6) while accumulating other PUFAs; exogenous DGLA repletion or D5D/FADS1 inhibition triggers ferroptosis selectively in senescent cells with healthy cells largely surviving; oral DGLA ethyl ester (400 mg/kg, 5 days) lowered senescent-cell burden and improved adipose, liver, and kidney markers in aged 22–24-month mice; and a FADS1 variant promoting DGLA accumulation in the human Kuopio Obesity Surgery Study was associated with lower adipose senescence markers. Boundaries recorded: mice and cell culture; the liver benefits are male-only in the paper and DGLA increased steatotic area; 400 mg/kg oral gavage is a research dose; the human leg is one genetic association in bariatric-surgery patients, not an intervention trial — so the post’s own hedge (“would be interesting if replicated in humans”) is accurate; “kill switch” is Johnson’s gloss; the post names no study.
- Promoted the GDF15 dunk-training disclosure as an attributed N=1 measurement claim with real context literature: “I’ve been training really hard to dunk a basketball. / Deadlift up 74% (245 → 425 lb) / Apparently my mitochondria are stressed by this. / We’ve been measuring GDF15 after workouts and it rises and falls according to the difficulty level of the workout.” (516 likes, 8 reposts, 79 replies; November 4, 19:21 UTC, with a photo). The arithmetic checks (245→425 lb is +73.5% ≈ 74%) and continues the tracked dunk-training storyline (October 25: 385 lb × 5 from floor, estimated 1RM ~445 lb). Context verified: Kleinert, Hempel, … Plomgaard, “Exercise increases circulating GDF15 in humans,” Molecular Metabolism 2018;9:187-191 (10.1016/j.molmet.2017.12.016 — 7 males, 60 min at 67% VO2max: plasma GDF15 +34% at exercise end and +64% at 120-min recovery, rising in every subject, muscle not the source), with GDF-15 responses intensity- and duration-dependent in trained adults (ACSM MSSE 2019, 10.1249/01.mss.0000561999.22383.5c). Boundaries recorded: his own series is unpublished N=1 with no assay, values, or dataset; acute exercise raises GDF15 in healthy humans by itself, so a post-workout rise is an expected stress response, not evidence of mitochondrial damage; “apparently my mitochondria are stressed” is his interpretation of a normal exercise-stress marker already tracked via the psilocybin-Parkinson’s relay; GDF15 also moves with illness, inflammation, and weight change.
- Promoted the music-walking relay with the study verified: “Walking to motivating music increased exercise by 15%” (1,593 likes, 62 reposts, 81 replies; November 4, 03:27 UTC, with a figure), quote-posting his own November 1 “listening to music while exercising is one of life’s greatest joys” post. Verified: Karageorghis, Mouzourides, Priest, Sasso, Morrish, Walley, “Psychophysical and Ergogenic Effects of Synchronous Music during Treadmill Walking,” Journal of Sport and Exercise Psychology 2009;31(1):18-36 (10.1123/jsep.31.1.18; PubMed 19325186) — N=30 walked to exhaustion at 75% heart-rate reserve under motivational synchronous, neutral synchronous, and no music; “motivational music elicited a 15% increase in treadmill endurance over control” (and 6% over neutral music), accounting for 38% of endurance-time variance. Boundaries recorded: the 15% is lab time-to-exhaustion on a treadmill, not free-living exercise volume; the effect requires synchronous stride-matched music, which “walking to motivating music” quietly drops; N=30 young Brunel students, one 2009 study; the post names no study; continues the November 1 “Music is a longevity therapy” storyline.
