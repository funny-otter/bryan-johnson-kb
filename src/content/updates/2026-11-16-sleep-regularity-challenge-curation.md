Its direction has the strongest evidence base of any sleep instruction this dashboard has audited: Windred, Burns, Lane, Saxena, Rutter, Cain, Phillips, “Sleep regularity is a stronger predictor of mortality risk than sleep duration: A prospective cohort study,” Sleep 47(1), January 2024 (10.1093/sleep/zsad253; PubMed 37738616) scored >10 million hours of accelerometer data from 60,977 UK Biobank participants (mean age 62.8) and found the top four regularity quintiles at 20%–48% lower all-cause mortality versus the least-regular quintile (most- vs least-regular: minimal model HR 0.52, 95% CI 0.45–0.60; fully adjusted HR 0.70), with regularity a formally better predictor of all-cause mortality than sleep duration. The mechanism lineage runs through Phillips, Clerx, O’Brien, Sano, Barger, Picard, Lockley, Klerman, Czeisler, Scientific Reports 2017 (10.1038/s41598-017-03171-4; 61 undergraduates, 30 days): irregular sleepers carried circadian phase delays equivalent to traveling two-to-three time zones westward (DLMO 00:08 vs 21:32), with regularity tracking academic performance (SRI–GPA r = 0.37).
- Boundaries recorded: the UK Biobank finding is observational in adults averaging 63 — the authors state sleep regularity “may be both a cause and marker of premature mortality risk,” and no trial shows a five-day challenge changes anything; the SRI measures 24-hour sleep/wake consistency while the post prescribes only bedtime; five days is shorter than every measured window (7-day accelerometry snapshots, 30-day diaries); “greatly improve your life” is an unfalsifiable promise in the same formula as the October 16 “I promise every part of your life will improve”; and sleep-hygiene-style advice alone is suggested against as a stand-alone therapy for chronic insomnia disorder (AASM, Edinger et al., J Clin Sleep Med 2021, 10.5664/jcsm.8988), so the challenge supports but does not treat established insomnia.
