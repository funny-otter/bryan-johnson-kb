# NTE age-decline claim curation

- Promoted Johnson’s August 29 nighttime-erection age-decline post as the window’s lead signal: age-binned nightly-duration tables (Age 20: 190 min, Age 50: 103 min, Age 60: 81 min, Age 75+: 50 min) repeating that “ED predicts cardiovascular events yrs before symptoms” and the “2x risk of heart attack and stroke over 4 years” figure.
- Added source-aware literature context: the age decline is genuinely well documented—Karacan et al., 1975 (Am J Psychiatry, doi:10.1176/ajp.132.9.932; 125 healthy males aged 3–79, EEG-verified) established age-normative NPT data; Schiavi et al., 1988 (J Gerontol, doi:10.1093/geronj/43.5.m146; 40 healthy men aged 23–73) showed NPT frequency and duration fall progressively with age independent of sleep changes; and Horita & Kumamoto, 1989 (189 healthy males aged 3–84) measured 189.6 min of total nightly tumescence at age 20 declining to ~62 min at age 70—putting Johnson’s age-20 anchor of 190 min almost exactly on published values.
- Kept the attribution boundary explicit: the post names no study; its intermediate bins (103/81/50 min) match no single published cohort (older-age values differ between series—Horita ~62 min at age 70 vs Karacan’s series ~102 min); the repeated flat “2x in 4 years” remains stronger than meta-analytic averages (Vlachopoulos et al., 2011: RR 1.48 for CVD, 1.35 for stroke); consumer NTE scores are not a validated clinical risk tool. Attributed normative-data claim-plus-context—not a validated risk prediction, a monitoring recommendation, or medical advice.
