- The sleep queuing-math post is the material sleep item — and the feed’s first quote-post of an AI assistant’s advice in the audited record: “Same math applies to sleep. / People mistakenly believe it's wasted time. / 6hr vs 8hr pushes your system near 100% capacity, multiplying errors, brain fog, and biological debt by 12x.” It quote-posts @Voxyz_ai sharing Gemini 4 Neon’s queuing-theory advice (“Deliberately run your time, energy, and resources below 80% capacity… congestion and delay do not scale linearly with how busy you are… at 80% utilization, your delay multiplier is 4. Push yourself to 90%, and it jumps to 9. Squeeze into 98%, and it explodes to 49. Packing that last 18% of ‘extra output’ into your week doesn't make you 18% more productive—it multiplies your wait times, error rates, and recovery cost by more than twelvefold.” — transcribed from the attached screenshot by OCR at curation time). Johnson’s “12x” and “near 100% capacity” borrow the AI’s figures directly, and the sleep mapping has a real laboratory anchor: Van Dongen, Maislin, Mullington, Dinges, “The Cumulative Cost of Additional Wakefulness: Dose-Response Effects on Neurobehavioral Functions and Sleep Physiology From Chronic Sleep Restriction and Total Sleep Deprivation,” Sleep 2003;26(2):117–126 (10.1093/sleep/26.2.117; 48 healthy adults aged 21–38 randomized to 4, 6, or 8 h time in bed for 14 days) found chronic 6 h nights produced cumulative, dose-dependent cognitive-performance deficits equivalent to up to 2 nights of total sleep deprivation — with subjects “largely unaware” of the impairment, exactly the errors-multiply-without-noticing shape the post asserts, and lapses near-linearly related to wakefulness in excess of 15.84 h. Boundaries: queuing theory is a metaphor, not a sleep model — no sleep study produces a “12x” multiplier, and the AI’s 4/9/49 delay multipliers describe M/M/1-style utilization curves, not brains; Van Dongen is 14-day restriction in 48 young adults on psychomotor tasks, not “biological debt”; “6hr vs 8hr” sits at or below Johnson’s own tracked October floor of 6.5–7.5 h ideal sleep.
- The phishing relay verifies number-for-number: “Sleep deprivation is a security risk. / Losing an hour of sleep during spring daylight saving time increased phishing click rates by 13% across 11,218 people.” traces to Schechtman, “Modest loss of sleep increases susceptibility to cybersecurity threats,” PNAS, published November 11, 2026 (10.1073/pnas.2622467123; PMID 43013551; PMCID PMC13778550; UC Irvine): 11,218 employees randomly assigned to receive a simulated predatory email on the Monday before versus the Monday after the spring DST transition, click rates 17.76% vs 15.66% (χ² = 8.87, P < 0.01; Risk Ratio 1.13, 95% CI 1.04–1.23) — the post’s “13%” is the relative increase on a 2.1-percentage-point absolute base; no differences in opening or reporting the email. Boundaries: one institution’s awareness program, simulated (non-malicious) emails, the spring transition only; the authors attribute the effect to sleep but DST also shifts circadian alignment and morning light — “sleep deprivation is a security risk” is the paper’s direction compressed to a slogan.
- The organ-aging relay traces to Mak, Krishnamoorthy, Zhang, Lin, Zheng, Jylhävä, Hägg, Tan, Kung, Cheung, “Organ-specific proteomic aging in Chinese adults: 20-year clinical outcomes and metabolomic underpinnings,” npj Aging, published November 19, 2026 (10.1038/s41514-026-00566-4): 185 Chinese adults from the Hong Kong Osteoporosis Study with 20.3-year median follow-up, 13 SomaScan-based proteomic aging clocks — each additional aged organ (age gap ≥ 1.5 SD) conferred a 56% higher mortality risk (HR 1.56, 95% CI 1.28–1.88), those with ≥3 vs none at 16.5-fold higher mortality (95% CI 5.34–50.78), immune age gap HR 1.95 (+95%) and brain HR 1.73 (+73%) — the post’s “biggest driver” and “second biggest” are the paper’s mortality-HR ordering. The tug-of-war lines track the paper’s metabolome-wide analysis: methionine inversely associated with conventional, organismal, and brain age gaps, and the taurine-hypotaurine pathway enriched across eight organ age gaps (after BMI/creatinine adjustment still enriched for heart, liver, brain, and kidney). Boundaries: n=185 with 28 deaths, a cohort enriched for high/low bone density and 73.5% women, associations not causes; the post’s specific organ pairings (methionine-younger-brain↔older-muscles, taurine-younger-vessels↔older-liver) sit in the figure/supplementary-level detail that the attached photo appears to read from, not in the paper’s main-text sentences — the inter-organ age-gap correlations the paper reports are mostly positive, with kidney and heart mostly negative, and the paper never frames organs as competing; the per-SD hazard ratios are per-standard-deviation relative risks, not absolute risk percentages.
- The Thanksgiving survival guide — “a thanksgiving survival guide to not feel awful / > eat turkey and vegetables first / > walk 10 minutes after the meal / > pie with people you love / > walk 23 miles after the pie / > skip alcohol / > stop eating 3 hours before bed” — carries two directives with real literature behind their direction: eating protein and vegetables before carbohydrate attenuates postprandial glucose (Shukla, Iliescu, Thomas, Aronne, “Food Order Has a Significant Impact on Postprandial Glucose and Insulin Levels,” Diabetes Care 2015;38(7):e98–e99, 10.2337/dc15-0429, 11 metformin-treated T2DM adults, glucose iAUC 73% lower when protein and vegetables came first; Shukla, Andono, Touhamy, Casper, Iliescu, Mauer, Zhu, Ludwig, Aronne, “Carbohydrate-last meal pattern lowers postprandial glucose and insulin excursions in type 2 diabetes,” BMJ Open Diabetes Research & Care 2017;5:e000440, 10.1136/bmjdrc-2017-000440, 16 adults, carbohydrate-last glucose iAUC 53% lower than carbohydrate-first), and light-intensity walking breaks attenuate postprandial glucose and insulin versus continued sitting (Buffey, Herring, Langley, Donnelly, Carson, “The Acute Effects of Interrupting Prolonged Sitting Time in Adults with Standing and Light-Intensity Walking on Biomarkers of Cardiometabolic Health in Adults: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis,” Sports Medicine, published February 11, 2022, 10.1007/s40279-022-01649-4; seven acute crossover trials, light walking Δ = −0.72 for glucose and −0.83 for insulin versus prolonged sitting, superior to standing breaks). Boundaries: the meal-order trials are in metformin-treated type 2 diabetes and prediabetes populations, not healthy adults at Thanksgiving scale; the walking meta-analysis measured repeated short breaks across a controlled day, not one 10-minute walk after one large meal; “walk 23 miles after the pie” is a joke (roughly a full day of walking); “skip alcohol” and “stop eating 3 hours before bed” are his own tracked bedtime guidance as generic direction, with no trial of this six-line list; the guide publishes no numbers.
