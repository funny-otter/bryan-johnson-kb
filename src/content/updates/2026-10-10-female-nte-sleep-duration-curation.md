During REM sleep, blood flow spontaneously increases”; his own counts (4-5 erections per night, averaging 3+ hours); his $275 wireless device vs. Kate Tolo’s $15,723.68 lab setup (5 cm probe + sensor light, wired to her leg, streaming to a laptop “that cannot be allowed to fall asleep”); “one of 1,900 biomarkers she’s tracking across her menstrual cycle.” Verified: the 2–5-year ED-to-coronary-event window matches the Princeton III consensus (PMC3498391: “a time window between ED onset and a CAD event of 2 to 5 years”), restated in Princeton IV; the female phenomenon is documented but thin — Karacan et al. (1970) first recorded the clitoral erection cycle during sleep, and Fisher et al. (1983; Archives of Sexual Behavior, 10.1007/BF01541556; 10 women aged 21–35, vaginal thermoconductance) found cyclic REM-linked engorgement in 95% of REM periods, though shorter, more often in non-REM, and less tightly REM-linked than in men. No published study links female nocturnal engorgement to cardiovascular or any health outcomes.
- Promoted the sleep-duration post (2108530005596877045, 3,040 likes — the day’s highest engagement): quote-posting BowTiedPhys’s “8 hrs was a psyop anyways” (2108181900107902986, 5,375 likes), Johnson endorsed “6.5-7.5 hr of sleep is ideal which means 7.5-8.5 hr time in bed assuming 85-90% efficiency,” conceding “The below graph is self reported from questionnaire” and adding “7 hr in bed is too short.” Verified: the ≥85% efficiency band matches the National Sleep Foundation’s 2017 sleep-quality consensus (Ohayon et al., Sleep Health; PubMed 28346153: “a sleep efficiency of ≥85% ... was judged as an appropriate indicator of good sleep quality”), and the arithmetic is internally consistent (6.5–7.5 h at 85–90% efficiency implies ~7.2–8.8 h in bed). Boundary recorded: the 6.5-hour floor sits below the AASM/SRS 2015 consensus that adults sleep 7 or more hours per night (Watson et al., J Clin Sleep Med, PMC4442216), and “8 hrs was a psyop” is the quoted account’s framing, not Johnson’s.
