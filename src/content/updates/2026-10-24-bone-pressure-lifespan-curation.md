- Promoted the bone-mechanosensing claim: “Mechanical pressure on shin bones extended mouse lifespan by 80 days (+9.4%). That’s the human equivalent of 7–9 yrs. Bone pressure triggers PIEZO1 sensors to broadcast anti-aging signals to the brain, liver, and immune system. If this translates to humans, my dunk training is flooding my entire body with anti-aging juice.” (83 likes, 17 replies, 2 reposts). Verification: the mechanism direction is real and 2026-active — Cai et al. (Nature Neuroscience, 10.1038/s41593-026-02422-w) show dynamic compressive tibial axial loading increases survival and recovery after stroke/TBI in mice and pigs via osteocyte PIEZO1 (IL-1R2, APOL11a, HSP70; serum BDNF, PF4, dopamine), and Zhang et al. (bioRxiv, 10.64898/2026.07.05.736555) define a mechano-bone-immune axis suppressing immunosenescence in aged mice and macaques, on a foundation of established Piezo1 bone mechanobiology (Li et al., eLife 2019, 10.7554/eLife.49631). But the headline figure is untraceable: the post names no study, its attached screenshot is Zhao et al. (Aging Cell 2026, 10.1111/acel.70687 — identified by OCR of the tweet image), which measures aged bone’s anabolic bone-mass response with no lifespan endpoint, and the identical “+80 days / ~9%” median-lifespan result (862→942 days, females only) belongs to Selman et al. (Science 2009, 10.1126/science.1177221) on S6K1 knockout — an mTOR-pathway genetics study with no mechanical loading. The “7–9 human years” is arithmetic on the mismatched figure; the “liver” leg of the brain/liver/immune triad has no identified source; and the mouse protocol in the real papers is an external ~15×-body-weight tibial compression device, not jumping.
