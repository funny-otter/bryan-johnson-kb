# LDL and nighttime-erection claim curation

- Promoted Johnson’s August 27 nighttime-erection post as the window’s lead signal: a self-reported “personal best” (4 hr 2 min of NTE at 93% strength from a 55,000-measurement database) calling NTE “a tier 1 longevity biomarker as critical to systemic health as VO2 max, resting heart rate, HRV, and blood pressure,” claiming low scores mean “2x risk of heart attack and stroke in the next 4 years,” and listing daily tadalafil 5 mg in his protocol.
- Added source-aware literature context: ED does precede and predict cardiovascular events—Vlachopoulos et al., 2011 (JACC meta-analysis, doi:10.1016/j.jacc.2011.06.024; 12 prospective cohorts, 36,744 men: RR 1.48 for CVD, 1.46 CHD, 1.35 stroke, 1.19 all-cause mortality) and the Krimpen population cohort (PubMed 17728804; 1,248 Dutch men aged 50–75: HR 2.6 for MI/stroke/sudden death only in severely reduced rigidity, over ~6.3 years)—but the post names no study, its flat “2x in 4 years” is stronger than the meta-analytic averages, consumer NTE scores are not a validated clinical risk tool, and daily tadalafil is a prescription medication requiring clinician oversight.
- Promoted Johnson’s August 26 LDL post as the second signal: “LDL cholesterol is one of the most actionable things from a blood draw,” citing a meta-analysis of 49 randomized trials and 312,175 people for ~23% fewer major vascular events per 39 mg/dL (1 mmol/L) reduction, plus a 13–14% six-month LDL reduction from viscous fiber, nuts, plant protein, and plant sterols.
- Verified both unnamed sources: the meta-analysis matches Sabatine et al., 2016 (JAMA, doi:10.1001/jama.2016.13985; 49 trials, 312,175 participants, 39,645 major vascular events: RR 0.77 per 1 mmol/L, 95% CI 0.75–0.79, pooled across statin and LDL-receptor-upregulating nonstatin therapies; mean follow-up 4.3 years), and the diet figure matches the Jenkins et al., 2011 portfolio-diet RCT (JAMA, doi:10.1001/jama.2011.1202; 345 hyperlipidemic participants over 6 months: −13.1% routine / −13.8% intensive LDL vs −3.0% low-saturated-fat control). The arithmetic is faithful; the gap is attribution—the post names neither study, and trial-population relative effects over years of treatment are not individual guarantees.
- Kept the attribution boundary explicit on both: attributed claim-plus-context, not a validated risk prediction, monitoring recommendation, lab-interpretation directive, diet prescription, or medical advice.
- Skipped as low-signal chronology: the August 27 “3x better at predicting the right cancer treatment when biology is tracked longitudinally” post (no study, dataset, or treatment context identifiable) and the truncated @saranormous retweet about “changing the norms around valuing life.”
