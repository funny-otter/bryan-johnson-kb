- The heart-rate disclosure is the material N=1 item — the feed’s first heart-rate-dipping post: “My heart beats 24,800 fewer times per day / > resting: 43 bpm / > lowest: 38 bpm / > 24/7 avg: 57 bpm / > night drop: 31% / > 24,800 per day is 8.8 million per year / > elite cyclists save 11,500 versus my 24,800 / Puts my heart in the top 1% of all men.” Its one mortality line — “any heart rate drop below 10% predicts higher mortality” — verifies exactly: Ben-Dov, Ben-Ishay, Mekler, Ben-Arie, Kark, Bursztyn, “Blunted heart rate dip during sleep and all-cause mortality,” Archives of Internal Medicine 2007;167(19):2116-2121 (10.1001/archinte.167.19.2116; 3,957 ABPM-referred patients, 303 deaths over 7.0 years): nondipping at all-cause mortality HR 1.45 (95% CI 1.14–1.84), lowest-vs-highest dip decile HR 2.67 (1.31–5.47); replicated in the general population (PAMELA, 2,021 adults: cardiovascular events HR 1.8, 1.13–2.86; PubMed 29517560) and treated hypertension (Eguchi et al., J Hypertens 2009: HR 2.37 for CVD events). Boundaries recorded: association in referred mostly-hypertensive patients, not evidence deepening the dip lowers mortality; the post’s arithmetic drifts (24,800 × 365 ≈ 9.05 million, not 8.8 million); “top 1% of all men” is the unpublished-percentile pattern.
- The plasma-proteome relay traces exactly to Kjærgaard, Kurgan, Gräf, Zhang, Wang, … Deshmukh, “Plasma proteomics identifies insulin resistance signatures predictive of cardiometabolic disease risk,” Nature Metabolism, published November 16, 2026 (10.1038/s42255-026-01662-1): 13 proteins + sex, HbA1c, TG/HDL at R² = 0.67 (validation 0.73 vs 0.49 clinical-only); T2D concordance 0.86 vs TG/HDL 0.74 in ~20,000 UK Biobank adults over 10-year follow-up; fatty liver 0.78 vs HbA1c 0.58; Inter99 beat HOMA-IR, all-cause mortality HR 0.56 (0.45–0.71); the 8-week HIIT cohort improved insulin sensitivity 30–40%. Boundary recorded: HbA1c still edged it for diabetes itself (0.88 vs 0.86); the panel is an Olink research platform, not an orderable test.
