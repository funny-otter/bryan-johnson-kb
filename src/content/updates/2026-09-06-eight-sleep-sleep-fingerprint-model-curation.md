# Eight Sleep sleep-fingerprint model, dunk-training PR curation

- Promoted one of the four September 4 posts. The Eight Sleep announcement became the window’s lead signal: Johnson “just launched an AI model based on sleep data… and it accurately predicts your age,” built with CEO Matteo Franceschetti, claiming a “sleep fingerprint” that identifies a sleeper from one night’s signal with 92.5% accuracy, estimates biological age within 3.3 years, and detects conditions including diabetes (0.852), heart failure (0.822), hypertension (0.810), sleep apnea (0.792), snoring (0.751), and general heart conditions (0.734), trained on 2.04 million hours from 136,575 participants with log-linear batch-size scaling (R²=0.982). This pass verified the claims against the underlying preprint—BCG-FM, Kjaer et al., arXiv:2606.07692—and every headline figure matches: the same pretraining corpus, 3.26-year biological-age MAE (lowest reported for any ambient, contactless modality), 92.5% Rank-1 identity retrieval at batch 4096, the same disease AUROCs (heart failure 0.822 on three independent external clinical cohorts), and R²=0.982. The dashboard publishes the claim with those boundaries: a vendor collaboration behind Eight Sleep’s membership “Biological Age” feature co-developed with Johnson’s Immortals, self-reported internal disease labels, small external cohorts, an unreviewed preprint, and a “diabetes better than Apple’s model” comparison that has no corresponding table in the preprint (its Apple-Watch PpgAge benchmark is age accuracy, 2.43-year MAE on a healthy cohort, not disease detection). Johnson’s own post concedes the limitations and calls it “a research milestone, not a diagnostic device.” Recorded as an attributed product-launch and research claim with verified preprint context—not a validated diagnostic, purchasing guidance, or medical advice.
