The LSD relay names no study but every line traces to one paper published the same morning — Savino, Liaci, Bertani, … Iorio, “Lysergic acid diethylamide reverses aging- and neurodegeneration-associated brain transcriptional programs,” Aging Cell, November 11, 2026 (10.1111/acel.70793; preprint 10.64898/2026.05.26.727809). Verified claim-by-claim: chronic LSD in the rat medial prefrontal cortex anti-correlated with age-ranked genes across integrated human prefrontal-cortex datasets (3,086 samples, ages 2 days–106 years; Fisher-aggregated p < 2.2 × 10⁻³⁰⁸); “stronger than exercise” is true only on the transcriptomic age-reversal ratio (chronic LSD average apr = 198 vs exercise/enriched-environment positive controls at largest average apr = 136); a separate acute mouse experiment (50–500 µg/kg) opposed aging signatures at every dose; and LSD restored amyloid-β-impaired MAP2-positive dendritic arborization in primary cortical neurons. Boundaries recorded: preclinical only (rats, mice, cultured neurons — the evidence record shows zero human LSD results), bulk-postmortem-tissue cell-mix limitation named by the authors themselves, signature reversal is a computational repurposing rationale rather than demonstrated slowing of brain aging, the post names no study, and LSD remains a Schedule I controlled substance in the U.S. outside regulated research.
- The GLP-1 relay is the feed’s most exactly verified research claim to date: “Lifting weights 3x a week while taking a GLP-1 preserved muscle, had double digit strength gains, and got rid of fat.” traces to the T-REx randomized controlled trial, “Resistance exercise to attenuate lean mass loss during tirzepatide therapy for obesity,” Nature Medicine, November 11, 2026 (10.1038/s41591-026-04752-5) — 120 adults 50–70 with overweight/obesity (no type 2 diabetes) on tirzepatide, randomized to thrice-weekly resistance exercise for 40 weeks (91% adherence). Verified number-for-number: lean soft-tissue loss −3.57 kg with training vs −4.67 kg without (P<0.001, ≈24% less; the university release rounds to 25%); strength +10.7% bench, +15.7% lat pulldown, +17.5% knee extension vs −8.4%/−6.5%/−9.0% declines without training (all P<0.001) — “double digit strength gains” is exact on all three lifts; fat-mass loss ~17–18 kg and statistically identical between arms, so the fat loss is the drug’s, not the lifting’s. Boundaries recorded: tirzepatide (GIP/GLP-1), not semaglutide; adults 50–70 with obesity, not Johnson’s regimen; DXA lean mass is not muscle quality; investigator-initiated trial with free manufacturer medication but no manufacturer role; and Johnson’s Immortals Rx sells GLP-1 medications — the same commercial adjacency recorded on the September semaglutide mouse-lifespan relay.
