# Interval-training mortality-claim curation

- Promoted Johnson’s August 25 interval-training post as the window’s lead signal: a concrete exercise prescription (3 workouts a week for 8 weeks, each four rounds of 4 minutes at 90–95% effort with 3 easy minutes between) paired with the claim of a “lower risk of dying from any cause by 11% in 8 weeks” and the closing line “your improved cardiorespiratory fitness is the risk reduction” (1,818 likes at re-read).
- Added source-aware literature context instead of repeating the figure uncited: the protocol is the Norwegian 4×4 from Helgerud et al., 2007 (Med Sci Sports Exerc, doi:10.1249/mss.0b013e3180304570; 40 moderately trained men, workload-matched, 3 sessions/week for 8 weeks: +7.2% VO2max for 4×4, 5.5% for 15/15, no change for moderate continuous groups), and the mortality arithmetic tracks Kodama et al., 2009 (JAMA meta-analysis, doi:10.1001/jama.2009.681; 33 studies, 102,980 participants: 13% lower all-cause mortality per additional MET of fitness, RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84–0.90).
- Kept the attribution boundary explicit: no trial has measured mortality outcomes from eight weeks of interval training; the 11% compresses an RCT fitness outcome plus a long-run observational association into an unattributed figure; the post names no study and its linked destination was not resolved in the bounded capture. Attributed claim-plus-context, not an exercise prescription or medical advice.
