- Promoted the three-tier sauna protocol with its population legs verified: “most people sauna incorrectly / > here’s three levels of sauna / > pick your fighter” (2,316 likes, 86 reposts, 203 replies; November 3, 20:21 UTC) — LEVEL 1 at 11 minutes/176°F, LEVEL 2 at 20 minutes/200°F with personal results, LEVEL 3 at core ~102.2°F/39°C “tracked via a pill.” Verified: Laukkanen et al., JAMA Internal Medicine, February 23, 2015 (10.1001/jamainternmed.2014.8187 — Kuopio cohort, 2,315 men, 4–7 sessions/week at sudden-cardiac-death HR 0.37, fatal CVD HR 0.50, all-cause mortality HR 0.60, with the 11-minute Level 1 figure being the paper’s own duration bin boundary) + Kunutsor, Laukkanen, et al., Age and Ageing, March 2017 (10.1093/ageing.afw212 — same cohort, dementia HR 0.34, Alzheimer’s HR 0.35 at 4–7 sessions/week). Boundaries recorded: one observational Finnish male cohort (self-selection, one-time baseline questionnaire, mean ~79°C sauna below the post’s 200°F Level 2, no randomization, no women); the Level 2 personal results (“10+ yr vascular age,” “87% less microplastics in blood,” “100% semen elimination”) are unpublished N=1; and the Level 3 dose is internally inconsistent within the post — “31 minutes at 200°F” in the protocol line, “this took 33 minutes” two paragraphs later, against the 56 minutes the original June experiment reported.
- Promoted the compute-medicine relay with the training corpus verified: “Medicine is a compute problem. / This model was able to predict your next health event. / Trained on 300M patients and 16B health encounters” (1,041 likes, 74 reposts, 68 replies; November 3, 23:47 UTC, with a figure). Verified: Waxler, Blazek, et al., “Generative Medical Event Models Improve with Scale,” arXiv:2508.12104 — Epic Cosmos holds 16.3 billion encounters over 300 million patient records from 310 health systems; Curiosity models trained on a 118M-patient/115B-event subset autoregressively predict the next medical event, with EHR integration announced for March 2027. Boundaries recorded: “Medicine is a compute problem” and “predict your next health event” are gloss — sequence modeling over billing-grade EHR events for contributing health systems, not individual prediction; the paper’s own limits (documentation error, non-Epic care gaps, discretized labs, 8,192-token context window, no prospective validation); the post names no model, paper, or vendor.
