- Promoted the semen-microplastics relay with both cited studies verified: “Up to 100% of men have microplastics in their semen / I got mine to zero. The first documented case. / A 2024 study detected microplastics in 100% of human and dog testicular tissue. / In another study, microplastics were detected in the semen and urine of all of 113 tested men from China… PTFE… proved the most lethal, creating 11% lower sperm concentration and 9% fewer total sperm. / Ten studies have measured microplastics in human semen. Five found 100% detection. / The rest from 84% down to 37%… So in short, 100% prevalence is plausible if not likely.” (1,428 likes, 65 reposts, 104 replies). Verified: Hu/Garcia/Campen et al., Toxicological Sciences, August 2024 (10.1093/toxsci/kfae060) — microplastics in all 47 canine and all 23 human testes, human mean 328.44 µg/g, PE dominant, authors’ limits: postmortem samples, refining methods, no causality — and the eBioMedicine 113-man multi-site China study (10.1016/j.ebiom.2024.105369) — microplastics in all semen and urine samples, PS/PP/PE most detected (the “packaging” polymers), PTFE exposure associated with lower sperm concentration (52.13 vs 58.32 million/mL, p=0.041; the post’s “11%” is 10.6% rounded) and total sperm count (188.90 vs 207.67 million; the “9%”), logistic OR 4.32 for reduced concentration; authors’ limits: cross-sectional, association not causation, fertility-clinic population, no exposure concentrations. The “ten studies / five at 100%” tally is consistent with the published semen-MP literature (the 36-man general-population Raman study; the 113-man eBioMedicine study; a 115-man Malaysian Raman cohort all at 100%; older LD-IR/FTIR work lower: 11/25, 34/45, 6/10) though the exact “84% down to 37%” endpoints match no single published tally; “Raman down to 1 micron” compresses instrument capability vs protocol reality. Boundary recorded: the “first documented case” of zero is a self-reported specialty-lab test with no published method — Johnson’s own reply concedes “This test is not widely available, sadly. I had a specialty lab create the test for me and conduct the analysis” (60 likes) — and field methodology reviews (Arch Toxicol 2026, 10.1007/s00204-026-04392-1) flag unstandardized detection and contamination risk that cut at “zero” and “100%” claims alike.
