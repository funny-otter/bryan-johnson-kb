- Promoted the young-stroke relay with the paper verified: “Strokes in young people used to be considered freak events. A new study found that young adults having strokes increasingly have the bodies of 60 yr olds. In stroke patients aged 18–35, those with 3+ major cardiovascular warning signs jumped by 50% in eight years. The culprits: high blood pressure, high cholesterol, obesity, and smoking.” (350 likes, 20 reposts, 56 replies, with an attached figure). Verified: “Temporal Trends in Vascular Risk Factor Burden Among Young Adults With Ischemic Stroke — The Florida Stroke Registry,” Neurology, March 25, 2025 (10.1212/WNL.0000000000213447) — 36,488 Get With the Guidelines-Stroke ischemic-stroke patients (January 2014–December 2022), 3,363 aged 18–35, with 3+ risk-factor polymorbidity rising from 10.9% to 16.4% in the 18–35 group (p=0.002): a 50.4% relative increase over exactly eight years, and the post’s four named culprits (hypertension, dyslipidemia, obesity, smoking) all sit on the paper’s nine-factor list; the 36–55 group rose 34.6%→41.9%. Boundaries recorded: the figure is the share of already-hospitalized young stroke patients carrying 3+ factors, not population stroke risk; the jump is relative (5.5 percentage points absolute); a Florida hospital registry is not a population sample; “a new study” is 19 months old and unnamed in the post; “bodies of 60 yr olds” is gloss the paper does not make (risk-factor counts, not biological age); and rising prevalence among stroke patients is association with the documented incidence rise, not demonstrated cause.
- Promoted the diet-timing immune-memory relay with the paper verified: “Avoid eating dirty during an acute immune reaction bc it can permanently lock in how immune cells react in the future. *viral infection, autoimmune flare, tissue injury…” (1,473 likes, 64 reposts, 58 replies, with an attached figure). Verified: Jiang, Tayeri, … Bapat, “Diet at disease onset determines tissue inflammatory memory,” Nature, published October 28, 2026 (10.1038/s41586-026-11181-4) — one day before the post, with UCSF’s same-week news release (“Could A Few Bad Dietary Choices Worsen Your Next Immune Response?”). Core verified: in mice, transient high-fat feeding confined to a 4-day peri-initiation window amplified imiquimod-induced type-17 skin inflammation in lean animals to severity comparable with chronic obesity (~2.1-fold ear thickening after one week); the same fat load outside the window had no impact, and switching obese mice to low-fat food during the window blunted the amplification. After recovery and return to low-fat food, a chromatin-poised tissue-resident memory CD4+ T-cell population persisted in previously inflamed skin, keeping rechallenges exaggerated out to 6.5 months — an effect transplantable with the skin patch itself, abolished by NLRP3 inhibition during the initial flare or CD4 depletion, and mirrored acutely in the lung. Boundaries recorded: mice and a psoriasis-like cream model; the memory is tissue-local (only previously inflamed ears overreacted, not the contralateral ear or the whole animal); durable to 6.5 months is not “permanent”; “how immune cells react” in general overstates a skin CD4+ T_RM population; the “viral infection, autoimmune flare, tissue injury” list extends the finding to triggers the paper did not test; and the corresponding author’s own caution is that a laboratory mouse diet “doesn’t map neatly onto what we eat.” The post names no study.
